### Interpretation (exploratory)

- **A stronger teacher did NOT produce a better student on "mass on the best move".** The weak-master-trained student puts *more* mass on Stockfish's best move than the Stockfish-trained student does. Counterintuitive and the headline.
- **The true master produced a better-*calibrated* student**: lower weighted CE and much less entropy collapse, on both yardsticks.
- **Mechanism (plausible, not proven): capacity binds, not teacher strength.** The weak master's moves are more stereotyped/easier to fit, so the student over-sharpens (entropy −0.81) — the *same* over-sharpening signature as the earlier target-rule/step-size work. Sharpening concentrates mass, which mechanically raises single-move mass (weak top-1 is inside Stockfish's top-5 86.8% of the time) without better imitation. Stockfish's best move is often a quiet move the 7.6k-param factored perceptron cannot represent, so its student stays diffuse — lower CE (CE rewards not being confidently wrong) but less mass on the single best move. **So the weak arm's "win" on best-move mass is largely a sharpening artifact, not evidence of better learning** — the same metric-validity lesson as before.
- Consistent with H20's literature (at low budget, tactical-material correctness binds; positional sophistication pays only once tactics are secured).
- **Caveats**: exploratory; one frozen corpus and held-out set; intervals cover init seeds only; measures **teacher imitation, not chess strength**; `search_weight=1.0` (the default that over-sharpens both arms) — a re-run at the sweep-recommended `search_weight≈0.125` is the natural follow-up and may change the picture.

- Consistent with H20 (capacity/tactics bind at low budget, positional sophistication does not pay) and with the anti-pattern "crediting a factor for what is really a budget/optimisation artifact".
- **This does not settle H25** (master vs *self-play*, which is still blocked on the retired self-play signal). It settles the *weak-vs-true* sub-question: no difference at a sane step.
- Still exploratory; one frozen corpus; seed-conditional intervals; imitation not strength.

### Trained arm (Stockfish labels, 583 random positions; RICH 91 → train 60 / held 31; POOR 492 → train 60 / held 167; 8 seeds; 4 epochs)

| arm | rich_held castling mass | poor_held castling mass |
|---|---|---|
| rich@sw1 | **0.5560** (untrained 0.0281) | **0.5467** (untrained 0.0257) |
| poor@sw1 | 0.0170 | 0.0149 |
| rich@sw0.125 | 0.1364 | 0.1196 |
| poor@sw0.125 | 0.0271 | 0.0245 |

- **The learner CAN be pushed to castle** (0.028 → 0.556 at sw=1.0; → 0.136 at sw=0.125). So the *features* are not the binding constraint for **expressing** castling.
- **But it is a blanket habit, not the teacher's judgement.** `rich@sw1` gains **+0.528** [+0.527,+0.529] on `rich_held` and **+0.521** [+0.520,+0.522] on `poor_held` — statistically identical. It castles just as much where Stockfish *rejects* castling.
- **How wrong is that?** In `poor_held`, castling is typically the **17th-best** move (median rank 17/20, mean 15.4) and **~670 cp** worse than best (159/167 gaps ≥100 cp; only 6/167 in the top-5). So the blanket bias is actively harmful — it is *not* the teacher's policy.
- The POOR-trained control does the opposite: slight castling *suppression* (−0.011).

### Conditioning test — the habit is un-conditioned on the teacher's rank

Added `--mode conditioning` (train on RICH; measure castling mass on 198 held-out positions bucketed by Stockfish's castling rank, MultiPV 20, depth 12):

| arm | rank 1 | 2–3 | 4–5 | 6–10 | 11–20 | >20 | Spearman ρ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| rich@sw1 | 0.587 | 0.525 | 0.544 | 0.575 | 0.551 | 0.530 | **−0.12** |
| rich@sw0.125 | 0.134 | 0.140 | 0.140 | 0.128 | 0.118 | 0.114 | **−0.25** |

- **Flat from rank 1 to rank >20.** The model casts at ~53–59% whether the teacher ranks castling 1st or 21st+. A model that had learned the teacher's *judgement* would show a strongly negative ρ; at most there is a very weak partial conditioning at the smaller step.
- This **closes** the alternative reading ("maybe it learned some conditioning, just noisily") — it did not.
- Artifact: `bench/h23_behavioural_conditioning.json`.

### Conclusion (exploratory)

- **The blanket-bias control is load-bearing.** Without the second held-out set, "a trained L3 castles (0.028 → 0.556)" would have been reported as a positive result. It is not: the model learned "castle whenever legal", not "castle when it is good".
- **H23 behavioural half resolves as: under-play is a signal-*coverage* gap (the pilot corpus has castling in the teacher's top-5 in only 3/500 positions), but "put castling in the corpus" is not a sufficient fix.** The binding constraint is the ability to **condition** castling on the position, not to express it — at this architecture (~7.6k params; `can_castle` is a single global scalar) and this corpus size (60 positions).
- The H25 feature-vs-signal fork resolves toward **signal** (a master-trained L3 *does* castle), with the caveat above.
- Caveats: imitation not strength; intervals seed-conditional on one fixed corpus/split; RICH/POOR is a binary selection.

### Follow-up: three controls on the "conditioning is the binding constraint" read

The previous section's conclusion was partly my own experimental design. Ran three confound checks:

1. **Corpus-contrast confound (partly real).** Every curated position has castling *legal*, so a RICH-only training corpus perfectly confounds "legal" with "targeted" — zero negative examples. Added MIXED corpora (`build_corpora`, two sizes: volume-matched `mixed_half` and RICH-dose-matched `mixed_full`). Discrimination (castling mass `rich_held` − `poor_held`) went **+0.009 (rich) → +0.047 (mixed_half@sw1)**, ~5×. So part of the flatness was the corpus. But +0.047 vs the ~0.5 a conditioned model would show — under a tenth.
2. **Explicit negative gradient does NOT create conditioning.** `apply_negative_castling` credits castling negatively wherever the teacher rejected it (124/244 updates confirmed). Result: castling suppressed **globally** — `mixed_half+neg@sw1` 0.539→0.325 on rich_held and 0.492→0.301 on poor_held. Discrimination does not improve (+0.025; −0.002 for mixed_full+neg). Mechanically expected: one logit per (from,to) pair, so credit generalises across positions.
3. **Feature probe (`--mode probe_features`).** The castling logit is a function of: `chan[:, from]`, `chan[:, to]`, geometry (constant), and the 9 global context scalars — nothing else on the board. Linear probe on those: held-out AUC **0.629 [0.53,0.73]** (context) / **0.606 [0.50,0.71]** (+local channels). Whole-board channel means: **0.743 [0.65,0.82]**. Signal exists on the board but is largely unreachable. CIs overlap → suggestive, not established.

**Structural fact (read from code, not inferred):** `train_on_targets` / `_apply_cached_targets` apply credit **only to moves in the target list**. Non-target moves get no gradient, so "don't play this" is learnable only via renormalisation. This bounds any corpus fix.

## Optimiser audit (2026-10-07) — L3 is the only trainable level without Adam

- **`torch.optim.Adam` already exists in the repo**: `torch_model.py:555` (L4) and
  `guided.py:705-706` (L5, separate decision + value optimisers). So "why wasn't
  Adam implemented" has a sharper answer than "we never did": **it was
  implemented one level up. L3 is the odd one out.**
- L3's update is `W += lr * credit * features` (`perceptron.py:583-595`,
  `lr=0.01`, `search_weight=1.0`) — plain SGD with a scalar credit. Not a
  gradient of a loss: `apply_credit` takes an externally-computed `credit`
  (outcome or `search_weight * gap`), i.e. a score-function/REINFORCE-style
  rule, so there is no gradient tensor for an optimiser to consume as-is.
- **Live inconsistency to note:** `tests/test_torch_model.py::test_logits_
  match_l3_exactly` is a *forward-pass* parity test. L3 trains by SGD, L4 trains
  by Adam, so any L3-vs-L4 comparison is already **optimiser-confounded** and
  the parity test does not cover it.
- Three real reasons L3 stayed on SGD: numpy-only constraint for L0-L3 (torch
  is an L4/L5 optional extra); the credit is not a loss gradient; and the
  **fixed-point quantised mode** — Adam's adaptive scaling can drive updates
  below `QUANT`'s grid, which is exactly the X5 trap already recorded for L3.5
  (`lr/64` → 0.4 grid units → `np.round` → zero; 5 of 8 tensors bit-identical).
- **Prediction, not a result (untested):** Adam's steady-state update ≈
  `lr·sign(g)` is roughly invariant to a *constant* rescaling of the gradient,
  and the max-rule bug was a ~3.2x aggregate target-scale error — precisely the
  class of error Adam is built to absorb. `max_scaled` (÷3.21) removed 89% of
  the CE damage, so a hand-rolled numpy Adam over the same credit should land
  near `max@0.125` (CE ~-0.004, entropy ~-0.010), not near `max@1` (CE +0.83,
  entropy -0.81). Caveat: the 3.21 is a per-position *mean*, not a constant, and
  Adam normalises per-parameter over time — so it should blunt, not necessarily
  eliminate. **Falsifiable and cheap on the existing frozen corpus.**

## Adam arm — run, and it REFUTES the "use a standard optimiser" intuition

- Added an `adam` arm to `bench/scripts/l3_target_rule_ablation.py`: `_AdamL3Trainer`
  routes the *same* `credit * features` through Adam's moment accumulators,
  keeping the max target rule, the credit and `lr` identical. `_array_key` keys
  moment state on `(id(base buffer), data pointer)` so row slices like
  `W_from[f_c]` get their own state (a plain `id()` would reset every step,
  since each slice is a fresh view object).
- **Pre-registered read** (written into the payload before the run): if Adam acts
  as a sane step, CE/entropy move toward `max@0.125` and `best/topk` stays near
  the ~0.40 share; if the overshoot is *aggregate* rather than per-parameter, CE
  stays near `max@1` or worsens and `best/topk` **drops**.
- **Result at 500 positions seen (seed-mean, 8 seeds, same frozen corpus):**

  | arm | CE delta | entropy | topk | best | best/topk | CE regressed |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | `max` (SGD control) | +0.8296 | −0.8094 | +0.0611 | +0.0261 | 0.276 | 107/128 |
  | `max_scaled` | +0.0945 | −0.1387 | +0.0267 | +0.0117 | 0.253 | 84 |
  | `sum` | −0.0006 | −0.0347 | +0.0137 | +0.0063 | 0.242 | 68 |
  | `adam` (lr 1.0×) | **+20.76** | −2.486 | +0.0788 | +0.0175 | **0.226** | **128/128** |
  | `adam@0.1` | **+11.10** | −1.803 | +0.0673 | +0.0125 | 0.216 | 123/128 |
  | `adam@0.01` | +0.0000 | −0.0000 | −0.0000 | −0.0000 | 0.227 | 6/128 |

  All three SGD controls reproduce the earlier run exactly, so the harness is
  correct.
- **Conclusion: Adam does not fix the artifact, it amplifies it ~25×.** The
  pre-registered discriminator fired the *aggregate* way: `best/topk` is lowest
  for both live Adam arms (0.226, 0.216) versus 0.276 for the SGD control —
  Adam *equalises* the push across ranks instead of shrinking it proportionally,
  so it raises set mass while giving the best move a smaller share.
- **Mechanism (this is the useful part).** SGD's step is `lr · gap · feature`,
  and `gap = p_target − p_model` → 0 as the model converges. That is what makes
  the update **self-limiting** and gives it a fixed point. Adam's step is ≈ `lr`
  regardless of gradient magnitude, so it **removes that damping** and keeps
  pushing at full force. The CE trajectory confirms it: `adam@0.1` goes
  +0.02 → +1.10 → **+11.10** (accelerating), where a self-limiting rule would
  saturate. Adam is scale-invariant on exactly the wrong axis.
- **The `adam@0.01` arm is NOT a counterexample — it is the X5 quantisation trap.**
  `lr = 1e-4` → Adam's step ≈ 1e-4 → ×QUANT(1024) = 0.102 grid units → `np.round`
  → 0, so the weights barely move (all deltas ≈ 0, 6/128 spurious regressions).
  It reproduces the L3.5 `lr/64` failure exactly. Do not read it as "Adam works
  at a small enough lr".
- **Honest gap:** lr scales 1.0 and 0.1 diverge; 0.01 is frozen by the grid. An
  intermediate lr is untested, so "Adam diverges at every workable step size" is
  supported but not exhaustively shown.
- **My earlier prediction to the user was wrong** and was corrected before the
  run: I had said Adam would "very likely have absorbed most of it". It is the
  opposite. The reason is that the mismatch is an *aggregate-across-moves*
  property of the target construction, not a per-parameter gradient-scale
  problem, and Adam is invariant to the latter only.

## Provenance guard: a byte hash was a false alarm; replaced with a behaviour digest

- `bench/scripts/l3_target_rule_ablation.py`'s guard reported `matches: False`
  against the pilot. Diagnosed, not assumed:
  - Not line endings — `perceptron.py` is pure LF (0 CRLF), and the LF-normalised
    hash equals the raw hash.
  - Not a semantic change — the pilot's recorded sha matches commit **`36d3add`**,
    current matches **`92d4cb9`** ("update method"), which is a **pure refactor**:
    `train_on_targets` extracted from `train_on_search_feedback`, same
    `p_target = w / best_w`, same gap, same credit.
  - **Verified executably, not from the commit message**: re-running
    `l3_heldout_per_position.py` with current code reproduces the pilot trajectory
    to 15 significant figures — top-k delta **0.061126762900030385** vs the
    pilot's **0.0611267629000304**, with CE 0.8295653470584132, entropy
    −0.8094216073318841 and 107 regressed positions all matching.
- **Fix:** added `_behaviour_digest(pilot)` — replays the first 32 cached records
  through the current update path and hashes the resulting weights. A refactor
  leaves it unchanged; a change to the credit, target rule or parameter shapes
  moves it. Verified stable across separate processes. Recorded *alongside* the
  byte sha, not instead of it: the sha proves which file ran, the digest proves
  what it did. Payload now also carries `byte_mismatch_is_known_benign`.
- **General lesson for the other guards** (`l3_heldout_diagnostic`,
  `l3_heldout_per_position`, `h23_behavioural` all carry byte-sha guards): a
  byte-level provenance guard cannot distinguish an inert refactor from a
  behaviour change, and firing on an inert edit trains the reader to ignore it.
  Behaviour digests are the right instrument.

### RESULT: captures condition, castling does not — pre-registered branch 1

- Shared held-out set (194 positions, where *both* classes are legal), corpora
  matched at 43 RICH / 43 POOR per class, 8 seeds, Stockfish d12 MultiPV 20.
  Metric is class mass divided by the no-preference share (1.0 = indifferent).

  | arm | rank 1 | 2–3 | 4–5 | 6–10 | 11–20 | >20 | rho | 95% bootstrap CI |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | castling@sw1 | 17.68 | 15.75 | 16.47 | 16.39 | 16.98 | 17.06 | **+0.022** | [−0.107, +0.181] includes 0 |
  | castling@sw0.125 | 2.67 | 2.54 | 2.71 | 2.68 | 2.66 | 2.64 | **−0.033** | [−0.163, +0.127] includes 0 |
  | capture@sw1 | 1.21 | 1.15 | 1.06 | 1.16 | 0.79 | 0.66 | **−0.192** | [−0.311, −0.023] |
  | capture@sw0.125 | 0.97 | 0.90 | 0.68 | 1.00 | 0.84 | 0.47 | **−0.204** | [−0.352, −0.065] |

  **Paired difference rho(castling) − rho(capture), same 194 positions:**
  sw=1.0 → **+0.206 [+0.003, +0.410]**; sw=0.125 → **+0.194 [+0.007, +0.381]**.
  Both exclude zero, at *both* step sizes.
- **Read: the pre-registered branch 1 fires.** Castling is flat at rho ≈ 0 at
  both step sizes; captures show a consistent weak negative rho. The difference
  is paired, directionally stable, and its interval excludes zero — so castling's
  flatness is **specific to a board-wide decision**, not a general inability to
  condition. That is the first evidence that the tier-2 gate is aimed at the
  right defect.
- **But the effect is marginal and must not be oversold.** The paired CI lower
  bounds are **+0.003 and +0.007** — barely off zero. And the capture effect is
  itself weak (rho ≈ −0.20, not the strongly negative profile a well-conditioned
  model would show). So the honest statement is: the learner conditions *weakly*
  even on a locally-decidable class, and *not at all* on castling. The receptive
  field is *a* binding constraint, not demonstrated to be the only one. The
  design note's own "not established" caveat survives, strengthened but not
  settled.
- **Design defect found and fixed mid-experiment.** The first version built a
  separate train/held-out split per class, leaving the classes evaluated on
  nearly disjoint positions — measured overlap was **4 of 48**, which made the
  paired comparison meaningless (and produced a spurious −0.225 "effect" with CI
  [−1.200, 0.000]). Fixed by splitting ONE shared held-out set over positions
  where every class is legal. This is why the earlier castling-only numbers
  (−0.12 / −0.25 on a different 198-position set) are **not** directly comparable
  to these; only the within-run paired difference is.
- Artifacts: `bench/h23_two_class_conditioning.json` (per-arm rho + CI, paired
  difference, per-position rows), `bench/h23_class_rank_variance.json` (583
  positions with per-class ranks). The primary endpoint and its interval are
  computed **in the script**, not in an ad-hoc shell command.
