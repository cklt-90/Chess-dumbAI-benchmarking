## Matched ablation of the target rule (user-approved: sum-normalized vs max-normalized)

- User approved a **separate, matched local ablation** of the update target: current `p_target = weight / max(weights)` (arm `max`, control) vs the alternative `p_target = weight / sum(weights)` (arm `sum`). Same frozen cached corpus, same held-out set, same 8 seeds; only the target mapping changes (`search_weight`, 300cp squash, credit path, checkpoint schedule all held constant). Deliberately not a bug fix and not folded into the pilot result.
- Built `bench/scripts/l3_target_rule_ablation.py` + `tests/test_l3_target_rule_ablation.py` (5 tests). Smoke confirmed the `max` arm reproduces the per-position diagnostic delta exactly (−0.011790531…), i.e. the control sits on the pilot trajectory. Runtime ~39 s full. `perceptron.py` sha still matches the pilot.
- **Result (checkpoint 500 positions seen, seed-mean): the target rule explains the entire aggregate disagreement.**
  - `max`: weighted CE **+0.830** (107/128 positions regressed), best-move mass +0.0261, top-k mass +0.0611, entropy **−0.809**.
  - `sum`: weighted CE **−0.0006** (68/128 regressed), best-move mass +0.0063, top-k mass +0.0137, entropy **−0.035**.
  - sum − max at 500: CE **−0.830**, entropy **+0.775**, best-move mass −0.0198. Same direction at 125 and 250 (CE diff −0.065 and −0.237; entropy diff +0.108 and +0.290).
- **Interpretation.** The `max` rule assigns target 1.0 to the best move and can assign up to k=5 units of target mass across the listed set, while the model's own probabilities sum to ≤1 over all legal moves — so every listed move sits persistently *above* the model's scale and the update pushes up on every position, indefinitely. The `sum` rule bounds total target mass to 1, matching the model's scale; the CE deterioration and entropy collapse vanish. Note the two rules **preserve the weight-vector shape** (identical ratios); they differ only in *scale*. So the mechanism is a target-scale mismatch, not a target-shape problem — an earlier test assertion I wrote claiming `sum` is "flatter" was wrong and was corrected to assert the scale property instead.
- **Caveats.** Still one frozen corpus and held-out set; conditional, exploratory, not a scale signal. The `sum` arm's small positive top-k mass (+0.0137) is far below the pilot's provisional MDE 0.05, so this does not demonstrate a *gain* — it demonstrates the *deterioration is removed*. A genuine improvement claim would need a pre-specified confirmatory design with corpus/held-out replication. The user's `max` rule remains production code; this ablation is not wired in.

## Confound ruled out: step size vs target rule (magnitude-matched third arm)

- Added a third arm `max_scaled` to `bench/scripts/l3_target_rule_ablation.py`: same `max` target rule, but `search_weight * 0.3742`. The factor is the corpus-average ratio of sum-arm to max-arm **total target mass** (max rule total = `sum(w)/max(w)`, mean **3.21** over 500 train positions; sum rule total ≡ 1.0), so `max_scaled` takes steps of the same aggregate magnitude as the `sum` arm while keeping the best move pinned at target 1.0. This separates "wrong target scale" from "step too big."
- **Result (seed-mean; seed-conditional 95% t intervals are tight, sd 0.002–0.03):**
  - CE Δ at 500: `max` **+0.830** [0.806, 0.853] · `max_scaled` **+0.095** [0.093, 0.096] · `sum` **−0.0006** [−0.001, 0.000].
  - entropy Δ at 500: `max` −0.809 · `max_scaled` −0.139 · `sum` −0.035.
  - CE regressed positions at 500 (of 128): `max` 107 · `max_scaled` 84 · `sum` 68.
  - Same ordering at 125 and 250.
- **Interpretation.** **Step size explains most of the effect**: matching magnitude alone (no rule change) removes ~**89%** of the CE damage (0.830 → 0.095). The `max` rule's aggregate target mass (~3.2× a probability distribution) makes every update ~2.7× too large; the policy over-shoots. A **small but non-zero target-rule residue remains**: `max_scaled` still leaves +0.095 nats at 500 with an interval excluding zero, where `sum` is −0.0006 with an interval touching zero. So both mechanisms are real; step size dominates, the target-scale mismatch adds a smaller component. This means an earlier framing ("the target rule is wrong") was **over-stated** — the dominant problem is the effective learning rate, not the normalization choice.
- **Caveats unchanged**: one frozen corpus/held-out set, exploratory, not a scale signal, not a bug fix, not wired into production. Do not promote to Kaggle. The correct next step, if pursued, is to state which mechanism is the intended object of study and design a confirmatory experiment accordingly — a step-size-only change is a different hypothesis from a target-rule change and must not be conflated.

## Objective framing: set-mass vs cross-entropy, and the gain decomposition

- The user asked which objective to pick — "maximize teacher set-mass, or keep CE/entropy stable?" Answered and quantified:
  - **Only cross-entropy is a legitimate training objective.** Set-mass is a **recall-style / satisficing** diagnostic: it rises when the policy spreads probability across the listed set, *even onto low-ranked members*. It is NOT safe to train on (invites the degenerate wrong-member solution the per-position diagnosis found). Entropy is not a goal at all — it is a diagnostic for detecting that degeneration.
  - Added absolute baseline/final levels to the ablation payload (`baseline_seed_mean_metrics`, `final_topk_mass`, `final_best_move_mass`, `final_best_over_topk_ratio`) so the decomposition is exact and reproducible, not inferred.
  - **Gain decomposition at 500 positions seen** (top-5 gain split into best-move vs non-best-move):
    - `max@1`: +0.0611 = best **+0.0261 (42.7%)** + non-best +0.0350
    - `max@0.5`: +0.0362 = best +0.0159 (44.1%) + non-best +0.0203
    - `max@0.25`: +0.0161 = best +0.0067 (41.6%) + non-best +0.0094
    - `max@0.125`: +0.0071 = best +0.0028 (40.0%) + non-best +0.0042
    - `max@0.0625`: +0.0055 = best +0.0022 (40.1%) + non-best +0.0033
    - Baseline: topk 0.1891, best 0.0429, best/topk 0.2268.
  - **Read:** the best-move share of the set-mass gain is **flat at ~40–44% across the whole sweep**. So most of the "set-mass gain" is *non-best* mass at every step size — it is largely the policy becoming less diffuse, not learning the teacher's ranking. This explains why the pilot's set-mass gain is not evidence of learning, and why shrinking the step loses the gain without losing anything real: the discarded "+0.061" was ~57% non-best mass that co-occurred with the CE blow-up.
  - The residual honest signal is the best-move mass (0.0429 → 0.0690 at max@1), and it tracks the step size. That is the quantity to optimize if a *real* improvement is wanted.
- Still exploratory and conditional on the one frozen corpus/held-out set; not a scale signal.
